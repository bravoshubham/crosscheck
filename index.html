ATC on the radio, as Crosscheck heard it

Said 11,000.Set 12,000.

Crosscheck heard the clearance on the radio, read the altitude selector through a camera, and spoke up before the aircraft got there.

Air traffic control cleared the 737 to 11,000 ft; the pilot dialled 12,000. Flying past a cleared altitude is an , and it can put two aircraft at the same height. Crosscheck does the job of the : it compares what was said, what was set and what the aircraft is doing, and calls out in standard words when they disagree.

Flight-test report

Every test was flown live in X-Plane 12's Boeing 737. Crosscheck only saw the screen and heard the audio; the simulator's own data was logged on the side, only to score each run. Every run is kept here, the misses included, and every recording replays from the repository without the simulator.

  1. Watch the film

    Three minutes. It is these pages and recordings, filmed frame by frame; nothing was staged for it. Play it

  2. Replay one flight

    Open the altitude bust and press Play: the radio call, the panel as the camera saw it, and the callout at the second Crosscheck made it. Open the recording

  3. Read the test cards

    One card per thing that goes wrong. Each square is one live run; a hollow square is a miss, and every card links to its recording. Go to the cards

Or run every recording yourself, on any computer, without the simulator or an API key: git clone https://github.com/bravoshubham/crosscheck && cd crosscheck && uv run python cli.py replay --all

What the camera sees

One camera looks at the captain's instruments, as a person in the right seat would glance across. A second looks at the flap gauge, and an optional third at the captain. Crosscheck never reads the simulator's data.

    Test cards

      How it was tested

      • Speech is real speech recognition: every line is synthesised, played as audio and transcribed live by Muse Voice Transcribe. Only what comes back is parsed.
      • The panel is read from screen captures of X-Plane 12's 737, by on-device text recognition, by Muse Spark, or on board by Muse Glimmer; never from the simulator's data.
      • On board, every model runs on the test computer, and each run is sandboxed with every network connection refused; its record shows the refused connection to api.meta.ai.
      • Each fault was flown 2 to 6 times, plus 15 minutes of normal flying. Flown in a simulator; not certified for operational use. The pilot-camera footage is generated: no real person is filmed.
