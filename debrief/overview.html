ATC, radio, as heard

Said 11,000.Set 12,000.

Crosscheck heard the clearance on the radio, read the altitude selector through a camera, and spoke up before the aircraft got there.

It is a monitoring pilot that only sees and hears, like the person in the right seat: what was said, what was set and what the aircraft is doing, checked against each other, with a callout in standard words when they disagree.

Flight-test report

Every scenario was flown live in X-Plane 12. Crosscheck saw only the screen and heard only the audio; the simulator's own data was logged only to score it. Each run below was scored automatically against that data, and every recording replays from this repository without the simulator.

    How it was tested, and what it does not show

    • Speech is real speech recognition: every line is synthesised, played as audio and transcribed live by Muse Voice Transcribe. Only what comes back is parsed.
    • The panel is read from screen captures of X-Plane 12's 737 by on-device text recognition or by Muse Spark; never from the simulator's data.
    • The pilot camera is generated footage: Muse Image photos of synthetic pilots, animated with LTX-2.5 and anchored on the labelled photos. No real person is filmed.
    • A simulator, not an aircraft. One aircraft type, one region, daylight, the X-Plane demo's default weather.
    • Short runs of one fault each, repeated 2–6 times, and one 15-minute false-alarm flight: enough to show the method works, not a measured false-alarm rate.
    • Not certified, not for operational use.
